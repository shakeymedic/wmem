ACT ON THIS NOW

SIMULATION Ring cutters: on tungsten, titanium and steel rings the Dolphin cutter succeeded in 62/62 cuts vs 41/59 for GEM-II, 5/59 for Raptor shears and 0/62 for a manual cutter; check what your ED holds.

WORTH KNOWING ON SHIFT

DIAGNOSTIC Aortic syndrome: the new 7-item ARC rule missed none of 484 cases in two validation cohorts but ruled out only 21% to 36% of patients and omits ADD-RS high-risk features; keep the RCEM 2026 approach.

DIAGNOSTIC PE D-dimer strategies: YEARS and PEGeD avoided imaging in 51% and 53% but sensitivity fell to 91.8% and 91.0% vs 98.3% for a fixed threshold; keep the NICE NG158 pathway.

DIAGNOSTIC CVST: a negative D-dimer (LR− 0.11, 95% CI 0.06 to 0.19) keeps residual risk under 1% only when pre-test probability is below about 8% (calculated).

RCT Bougie vs stylet: in 188 difficult trauma airways intubated with a C-MAC, first-pass success was 71.3% vs 67.0% (difference 4.3 points, 95% CI −8.9 to 17.2).

COHORT Opioid cardiac arrest: good neurological outcome 7.2% vs 8.3% (aOR 1.02, 95% CI 0.77 to 1.35); do not lower the ceiling of care on that basis.

KNOW FOR NEXT TIME

UK ED waits (England, 6.7 million attenders): 12 hours vs 2 hours in the ED was associated with 2.1 times the adjusted odds of death within 30 days (95% CI 2.02 to 2.28), 1.7 after adjusting for admission; an association, not a count of deaths caused.

SR/MA Intranasal opioids: similar pain relief to IV morphine at 20 and 30 minutes and the same rescue analgesia (RR 1.00, 95% CI 0.81 to 1.25), but serious adverse events RR 1.87 (0.97 to 3.59), so harm is not excluded; no diamorphine trials.

SR/MA IV paracetamol with opioids: 0.65 NRS points lower at 30 minutes (95% CI 0.19 to 1.12), not significant without the largest trial.

SR/MA Dual cardioversion: 79% of refractory AF converted, but with no comparator and 10 of 14 in the only modern biphasic cohort.

RCT Radial nerve block: pain during reduction no different from a haematoma block (median 6 vs 8, p=0.29), the registered primary outcome.

PAEDS Influenza: antivirals within 48 hours linked to lower odds of hospitalisation (aOR 0.19, 95% CI 0.14 to 0.27), but against mostly late treatment; UKHSA criteria stand.

RCT Mild LVO stroke: in 300 perfusion-selected patients with NIHSS under 6, thrombectomy raised mRS 0–1 at 90 days to 69.4% from 50.0% (NNT 6), with serious procedural complications in 13.2% (abstract only).

ILCOR 2026: No Survival Benefit From Thrombolysis in CPR (3 RCTs), IM Adrenaline Not Endorsed, Paediatric Post-ROSC Fever Prevention Now Strong

Bray JE, Djarv T, Drennan IR, et al. 2026 International Liaison Committee on Resuscitation Consensus on Science With Treatment Recommendations: Summary from the Basic Life Support; Advanced Life Support; Pediatric Life Support; Neonatal Life Support; Education, Implementation, and Teams; and First Aid Task Forces. Resuscitation. 2026 (online 5 November 2026):111328; copublished in Circulation. doi: 10.1016/j.resuscitation.2026.111328. PMID: 42988037. Source route: PDF via BMA Library (supplied by Jake). Appraised from full text

ILCOR’s annual summary rests on 16 GRADE-rated systematic reviews. It recommends against routine thrombolysis during CPR (strong; 3 RCTs, 1,299 patients, moderate-certainty evidence of no survival or neurological benefit, low-certainty evidence of more bleeding with no difference in symptomatic intracranial haemorrhage; the pulmonary embolism recommendation is unchanged) and against starch solutions, and suggests against routine fluids in undifferentiated non-traumatic arrest. It finds insufficient evidence to add IM adrenaline (one before-after study) or to prefer video laryngoscopy (3 RCTs: first-pass RR 0.88, 95% CI 0.63 to 1.22), and suggests a standard adult bag and charging a manual defibrillator during compressions. For comatose children after ROSC it recommends active temperature management that prevents temperatures above 37.5 °C (strong, low certainty; upgraded from a weak 2022 recommendation).

Aortic Syndrome Rule-Out Criteria (ARC; Derived From 15,196 Cases, Validated in 9,944 ED Patients): No Case Missed, but Only 21% to 36% Ruled Out

Ohle R, Pai CW, Bima P, et al. Acute aortic syndrome rule-out criteria: development and external validation. Eur Heart J. 2026 (online 10 November). doi:10.1093/eurheartj/ehag875. PMID: 43006790. Source route: open-access publisher full text (read in Jake’s Chrome); supplement not seen. Background: a New Zealand external validation (n=1,000) of the ADvISED ADD-RS plus D-dimer strategy ran in Issue 33; this issue’s Core Revision covers aortic dissection. Appraised from full text

A seven-item rule (age over 60, abrupt pain, severe pain, hypotension or shock, neurological deficit, pulse deficit, known aortic disease; negative only if all are absent) was derived from 15,196 confirmed acute aortic syndromes (AAS) in the IRAD registry and applied retrospectively to two prospective ED cohorts, with AAS on imaging or follow-up as the reference standard. In the pooled ADvISED/PROFUNDUS cohort (n=4,598, enrolled on clinician suspicion, prevalence 10.2%) ARC missed none of 470 AAS (sensitivity 100%, 95% CI 99.2 to 100; specificity 23.7%, 95% CI 22.4 to 25.0), and in the UK DAShED cohort (n=5,346, prevalence 0.3%) it missed none of 14 (sensitivity 100%, 95% CI 76.8 to 100; specificity 36.4%, 95% CI 35.1 to 37.7). Placed in front of ADD-RS plus D-dimer in ADvISED/PROFUNDUS, it cut D-dimer tests by 25.2% (95% CI 23.8 to 26.6) and CT angiograms by 10.1% (95% CI 8.9 to 11.3) at an unchanged pathway sensitivity of 98.9%.

Suspected Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis (SR/MA, 33 Studies, 4,056 Patients): D-dimer LR− 0.11 and CT Clot Density LR+ 24.4; the Double-Negative LR− 0.02 Is Modelled, Not Observed

J Emerg Med. 2026 (journal pre-proof). doi:10.1016/j.jemermed.2026.09.019. No PMID yet. PROSPERO CRD42024562256. Source route: PDF via BMA Library (supplied by Jake); no expert review found by 12 November. Appraised from full text

Diagnostic SR/MA (Milan) of adults with suspected CVST, with CT venography, MR venography or DSA as reference; 22 of 33 studies were diagnostic case-control designs and only 6 were ED-only. Venous sinus density around 63 HU on non-contrast CT (cut-off chosen post hoc; 10 studies, 1,666 patients) had sensitivity 80.6% and specificity 96.7%, LR+ 24.4 (8.2 to 73.8). No study evaluated the combined strategy; the combined LR− of 0.02 assumes the tests are independent, although the only study measuring both found a correlation of r = 0.5 to 0.6.

Intranasal Opioids vs IV Morphine (SR/MA, 10 RCTs, 1,164 Patients): Similar Pain at 20 and 30 Minutes and Rescue Analgesia RR 1.00, but Serious Adverse Events RR 1.87 (0.97 to 3.59) and No Diamorphine Trials

Fida L, Alahmed H, Almutairi AS, et al. Comparative efficacy and safety of intranasal opioids vs. intravenous morphine for acute moderate-to-severe pain: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials. J Emerg Med. 2026;91:158–172. doi:10.1016/j.jemermed. 2026.09.021. PMID: 42994114. PROSPERO CRD420261420685. Source route: PDF via BMA Library (supplied by Jake; open access, CC BY); forest-plot values read from Figures 3 and 4; no expert review found by 12 November. Appraised from full text

Meta-analysis of 10 RCTs (2007 to 2025; USA, Australia, Belgium, Norway, France) comparing intranasal fentanyl (5 trials), sufentanil (3) or morphine (2) with IV morphine in adults and children with acute moderate-to-severe pain in ED, prehospital, procedural and postoperative settings. The primary outcome, change in pain score, came from 6 trials and did not differ at 20 minutes (SMD −0.05, 95% CI −0.24 to 0.14; 556 patients) or 30 minutes (SMD −0.05, −0.24 to 0.15; 406 patients). Rescue analgesia was the same (RR 1.00, 0.81 to 1.25; 8 trials). Serious adverse events were more frequent with intranasal opioids without reaching significance (RR 1.87, 0.97 to 3.59), as was dysgeusia (RR 2.74, 1.00 to 7.49).
