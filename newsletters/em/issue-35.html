BOTTOM LINE UP FRONT — 22 OCTOBER 2026

ACT ON THIS NOW

ABBOTT FSN i-STAT hs-TnI (Abbott FSN, MHRA-listed): lots beginning A24, A25, B25, B26, C26 or D26 can read falsely high from macrotroponin or heterophile antibodies; if a raised result does not fit the patient, repeat on a laboratory assay or an A26 lot rather than act on serial cartridge results.

WORTH KNOWING ON SHIFT

DIAGNOSTIC Miosis: 1 in 4 opioid-positive patients with a respiratory rate under 10 had normal or dilated pupils (LR− 0.46); treat the respiratory rate, not the pupils.

DIAGNOSTIC HINTS Plus: sensitivity 80.6% for a central cause in acute vestibular syndrome; a “peripheral” result is not a stand-alone stroke rule-out.

REVIEW Necrotising infection: LRINEC 6 or more has 68.2% sensitivity, so a low score does not rule it out.

KNOW FOR NEXT TIME

RCT POC troponin (lead): 47 minutes shorter ED stay (95% CI 33 to 61), safety unchanged; no POC hs-troponin is on NICE’s recommended list.

RCT Intermediate-high-risk PE (PRAGUE-26): catheter-directed alteplase cut the 7-day composite from 6.8% to 0.7%, mostly decompensation; both intracranial bleeds were in that arm.

RCT Vasopressin (VASSPR, n=3,959): starting at noradrenaline 0.1 vs 0.4 µg/kg/min did not change 28-day mortality (31.8% vs 33.0%).

SR/MA Oxygen after ROSC: conservative targets did not lower mortality (RR 0.96) but caused more hypoxaemia (RR 1.65); keep to SpO2 94–98%.

SR/MA Peripheral vasopressors: minor complications in 2.3% (2.6% with noradrenaline) across 33,060 catheters, mostly observational data.

PAEDS Hypothermia: a first temperature of 36°C or less in children carried adjusted odds of death of 20.15, though absolute mortality was about 0.5%.

ICare-FASTER (Stepped-Wedge Cluster RCT, 6 EDs, 52,601 Presentations): Point-of-Care hs-Troponin I Cut ED Stay by 47 Minutes, With 30-Day MI or Cardiac Death Unchanged (0.39% vs 0.39%)

Than M, Pickering JW, Joyce LR, et al. ICare-FASTER (Improving Care by Faster Risk Stratification Through Use of High-Sensitivity Point-of-Care Troponin in Patients Presenting With Possible Acute Coronary Syndrome in the Emergency Department): A Stepped-Wedge Cluster-Randomized Trial. Circulation. 2026 (published online 19 October 2026). doi:10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.126.080866. PMID: 42895015. ACTRN12619001189112. Source route: PDF via BMA Library (supplied by Jake); open access (CC BY-NC-ND). Appraised from full text

Pragmatic stepped-wedge cluster-randomised trial in 6 New Zealand hospitals (February 2023 to January 2025), with each hospital crossing over from control to intervention. All adults tested for possible MI were included: 21,209 control and 31,392 intervention presentations. The comparator was each site’s existing accelerated diagnostic pathway (0 and 2 hour sampling with EDACS) with a central laboratory hs-troponin; the intervention was the same pathway with nurse-run whole-blood Siemens Atellica VTLi hs-troponin I (low-risk threshold 7 ng/L). The primary outcome, ED length of stay, fell by 13% (95% CI 9% to 16%), or 47 minutes (95% CI 33 to 61) from a control mean of 376 minutes. 30-day MI or cardiac death in patients discharged from the ED was 76 of 19,271 (0.39%) vs 50 of 12,906 (0.39%), a difference of 0.01 percentage points (95% CI −0.14 to 0.15).

PRAGUE-26 (RCT, n=558): Catheter-Directed Alteplase in Intermediate-High-Risk PE Cut the 7-Day Composite From 6.8% to 0.7%, Mostly Decompensation; Both Intracranial Bleeds Were in the CDT Arm

Kroupa J, Radvan M, Mrozek J, et al. Catheter-Directed Thrombolysis in Intermediate-High-Risk Pulmonary Embolism. N Engl J Med. 2026 (published online 31 August 2026). doi:10.1056/NEJMoa2608012. PMID: 42678032. NCT05493163. Funded by the Ministry of Health of the Czech Republic and Charles University. Source route: NEJM author accepted manuscript (supplied by Jake); JournalFeed Article of the Day 21 Oct; Critical Care Reviews. Appraised from full text

Investigator-initiated open-label RCT at 11 Czech tertiary cardiovascular centres (October 2022 to March 2026). 558 haemodynamically stable adults aged 18 to 80 with intermediate-high-risk PE (sPESI 1 or more, RV/LV ratio 0.9 or more, raised troponin or natriuretic peptide; 49.5% transferred in) were randomised. The intervention was conventional catheter-directed thrombolysis, alteplase 1 mg bolus then 1 mg/h per catheter to 20 mg (10 mg if unilateral) over about 9 hours with reduced-intensity heparin; the comparator was anticoagulation alone with UFH or LMWH. The primary outcome, a 7-day composite of death, recurrent PE or cardiorespiratory decompensation, occurred in 2 of 280 (0.7%) vs 19 of 278 (6.8%): risk difference −6.1 percentage points (95% CI −9.2 to −3.0), RR 0.10 (95% CI 0.02 to 0.44). Decompensation drove it (0.7% vs 5.4%); deaths by day 7 were 0 vs 4 (1.4%). BARC 2 or higher bleeding at 7 days was 4.6% vs 5.0%, GUSTO major 1.4% vs 2.2%, and intracranial haemorrhage 2 (0.7%) vs 0.

INFORMING PRACTICE OBSERVATIONAL

Outpatient Low-Risk PE (Single-Centre Implementation Study, 1,810 Patients): ED Discharge of Low-PESI Patients Rose From 5.1% to 33.3%, and to 75% in a Post Hoc Hestia-Negative Subgroup

Greineder CF, Mefford V, O’Hare C, et al. Adoption of Outpatient Management of Acute Low-Risk Pulmonary Embolism. Ann Emerg Med. 2026 (published online October 2026). doi:10.1016/j.annemergmed.2026.08.738. PMID: 42878715. Source route: PDF via BMA Library (supplied by Jake); open access (CC BY). Appraised from full text

Before-and-after implementation study in one US quaternary academic ED, comparing a preimplementation period (October 2016 to December 2019) with three successive years from March 2021 to March 2024. The intervention had four parts: clinician education, an electronic record nudge triggered by an automated PESI of 85 or less, an order set with the first DOAC dose in the ED and social-work medication help, and a follow-up clinic within 7 to 10 days. Of 1,810 patients with acute PE, 705 (39.0%) had a low PESI; the primary outcome, discharge from the ED, rose from 17 of 331 (5.1%) to 45 of 135 (33.3%) in year III (unadjusted OR 9.24, 95% CI 5.04 to 16.92). In a post hoc analysis restricted to low-PESI patients without modified Hestia criteria it rose from 17 of 189 (9.0%) to 45 of 60 (75%); there were no recurrent VTE or deaths within 30 days of any ED discharge.

UK practice: NICE NG158 (2020, updated 2023) rec 1.2.1: consider outpatient treatment for suspected or confirmed low-risk PE using a validated risk stratification tool; rec 1.2.4: agree a monitoring and follow-up plan with written information, direct contact details for a thrombosis team and out-of-hours advice. Applying exclusion criteria on top of PESI is the combination this paper arrives at post hoc.

VASSPR (Cluster Crossover RCT, n=3,959): Starting Vasopressin at Noradrenaline 0.1 vs 0.4 µg/kg/min Did Not Change 28-Day Mortality (31.8% vs 33.0%)

Peltan ID, Groat D, Jacobs JR, et al. Effect of Vasopressin Initiation Threshold on Septic Shock Mortality: The VASSPR Cluster Crossover Randomized Strategy Trial. JAMA. Published online 14 October 2026. doi:10.1001/jama.2026.21333. PMID: 42872917. NCT06217562. Source route: PDF via BMA Library (supplied by Jake); PulmCrit Hot Take 15 October 2026. Appraised from full text

Pragmatic, open-label, cluster-randomised, cluster-crossover trial at 13 hospitals in one US health system (Utah and Idaho, February 2024 to July 2025). Every adult given a vasopressor for septic shock was enrolled; hospitals switched monthly between a strategy recommending fixed-dose vasopressin (0.03 units/min) once other vasopressors reached 0.1 µg/kg/min of noradrenaline or equivalent and one recommending it at 0.4 µg/kg/min. The primary outcome, 28-day all-cause mortality, was 31.8% (628/1,975) with the lower threshold vs 33.0% (655/1,984) with the higher threshold (adjusted marginal OR 0.98, 95% CI 0.87 to 1.06; adjusted risk difference −0.4 percentage points, 95% CI −2.7 to 1.3). Kidney replacement therapy-free days, the key secondary outcome, did not differ (median 29 in both; adjusted OR 1.01, 95% CI 0.91 to 1.14). The strategies separated only partly (table); the one safety signal, more cardiogenic shock with the lower threshold, was exploratory and not adjusted for 16 other outcomes.

Peripheral Vasopressors (SR/MA, 49 Mostly Observational Studies, 33,060 Catheters): Minor Complications in 2.3%; 1 Major Event in 29,596 Short Cannulas

ZhangJian SJ, Niu KY, Chen CB, et al. Incidence of Adverse Events in Peripheral Intravenous Vasopressor Use: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. JAMA Netw Open. 2026;9(3):e260710. doi:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.0710. PMID: 41838001. PROSPERO-registered (August 2025; number not given in the PMC text). Source route: PMC full text (PMC12993702); SGEM #522 (30 September 2026). Appraised from full text

Systematic review of adults given vasopressors through peripheral cannulas or midline catheters for hypotension, shock or critical illness (49 studies, 1997 to 2025; 9 in the ED). Only 4 were RCTs; 31 were retrospective and 14 prospective observational studies, and none had a central-line comparison in the main analysis, so the results are single-arm incidence rates. Minor adverse events (pain, swelling, infiltration, extravasation, thrombophlebitis) occurred in 2.3% of catheters (95% CI 1.5% to 3.7%; I² 95.2%), and 2.6% with noradrenaline (95% CI 1.4% to 4.7%). Short peripheral cannulas had 1 major event (tissue necrosis needing fasciotomy) in 29,596 catheters (0.003%; the pooled estimate rounds to 0.0%), while all 30 venous thromboembolisms were in the 4 midline studies (1,126 catheters; pooled 1.4%, 95% CI 0.4% to 5.4%).

REBEL EM on CRAO: Two Small RCTs Show No Significant Visual Benefit From IV Thrombolysis (TenCRAOS 20% vs 24% Vision Recovery With Aspirin)

DiBello J. Clinical Conundrum: What is the Role of Thrombolysis in Central Retinal Artery Occlusion? REBEL EM, 19 October 2026. rebelem.com/crao. Trials: Ryan SJ, et al. TenCRAOS. N Engl J Med. 2026;394(5):442–450. doi:10.1056/NEJMoa2508515. PMID: 41604638. Préterre C, et al. THEIA. Lancet Neurol. 2025;24(11):909–919. doi:10.1016/S1474-4422(25)00308-4. PMID: 41109232. Source route: REBEL EM RSS feed. Appraised from full text

REBEL EM concludes that routine IV thrombolysis is not supported for acute non-arteritic CRAO, despite the 2021 AHA statement that suggested considering it within 4.5 hours on the basis of observational data. In TenCRAOS (78 patients, 16 sites, six countries), vision recovery at 30 days occurred in 20% with tenecteplase 0.25 mg/kg vs 24% with aspirin 300 mg (risk difference −3.7 percentage points, 95% CI −22.0 to 14.7), with more adverse events and one fatal intracranial haemorrhage after tenecteplase. In THEIA (70 patients, France), alteplase did not significantly improve visual acuity compared with aspirin; the trial was likely underpowered. The post recommends rapid diagnosis, ophthalmology and neurology input, stroke work-up and secondary prevention instead.

EM Cases Ep 224: A Four-Step Approach to Episodic Vertigo

Lin K, Johns P, Targonsky E, Helman A. Vertigo Part 1: Approach to Episodic Vertigo. Emergency Medicine Cases, Episode 224, October 2026 (posted 20 October 2026). emergencymedicinecases.com/episodic-vertigo. Key reference: Edlow JA, et al. GRACE-3. Acad Emerg Med. 2023;30:442–486. Source route: EM Cases RSS feed.

The episode gives a four-step approach: screen for central and systemic red flags (including new inability to sit, stand or walk unaided, and nystagmus that is vertical or changes direction at rest); classify the syndrome by timing and triggers using the GRACE-3 framework; choose the test that matches the syndrome; then treat and decide disposition. For brief, position-triggered episodes without nystagmus at rest, perform a Dix-Hallpike, waiting 30 to 60 seconds before calling it negative, and treat posterior canal BPPV with an Epley manoeuvre rather than vestibular suppressants. The experts warn that HINTS and HINTS Plus are for continuous vertigo with spontaneous nystagmus and should not be used for episodic positional vertigo; worsening with movement does not distinguish peripheral from central causes. Routine neuroimaging is not advised for typical BPPV without red flags. For a UK ED: Use Dix-Hallpike and Epley for triggered episodic vertigo, and keep HINTS for continuous vertigo with nystagmus.

EMCrit 436: The Haemodynamically Neutral Intubation for the Shocked Patient Who Is Likely to Arrest With RSI

Weingart S. EMCrit 436: When you RSI shocked patients, they tend to die (the hemodynamically neutral intubation part 2). EMCrit, 18 October 2026. emcrit.org. Source route: EMCrit RSS feed.

Weingart describes the haemodynamically neutral intubation: keep the patient breathing spontaneously, because switching to positive-pressure ventilation reduces venous return, and avoid paralysis unless it is unavoidable. Candidates are right heart failure or pulmonary hypertension, tamponade, and patients on high-dose or multiple pressors with a MAP still in the 50s; those whose pressure corrects easily get ketamine-first delayed sequence intubation instead. The approach uses an arterial line, PEEP of 5 to 8 for cardiogenic shock or shunt (none for exsanguinating trauma), 3 minutes of preoxygenation, ketamine 20 to 30 mg then 20 mg aliquots until dissociated, video laryngoscopy with a bougie, and rocuronium with sugammadex ready. After intubation he advises a pressure-support mode at the pre-intubation PEEP and small ketamine pushes, not standard sedation infusions. For a UK ED: Nothing yet as protocol; a reason to identify the very high-risk shocked airway early and plan with senior anaesthetic and ICU colleagues.
