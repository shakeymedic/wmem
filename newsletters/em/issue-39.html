Emergency Physician Echo for Regional Wall Motion Abnormalities (Diagnostic SR/MA, 9 Studies, 1,191 Patients): Sensitivity 86% and Specificity 85% Against Cardiology Echo, but the Target Was a Wall Motion Abnormality, Not ACS

Neto ES, Scapin M, Verdun PA, et al. Identification of regional wall motion abnormalities by emergency physicians in acute coronary syndromes: systematic review and meta-analysis. West J Emerg Med. 2026;27(6):1703–1713. doi:10.5811/westjem.64752. PMID: 43023765. PROSPERO CRD420261292335. Source route: PubMed sweep of EM journals; open-access full text from PMC (PMC13761796, CC BY); supplement not seen; no expert review found by 19 November 2026. Background: an earlier SR/MA of cardiac ultrasound by any operator for acute myocardial ischaemia in the ED (29 studies, 5,043 patients; sensitivity 79.3%, specificity 87.3%, very low certainty), which this review does not cite: Scand J Trauma Resusc Emerg Med 2024;32(1):19, doi:10.1186/s13049-024-01192-3. Appraised from full text

Diagnostic SR/MA (searched to 16 December 2025) of adults in the ED with chest pain or suspected ACS or MI whose cardiac ultrasound was performed or interpreted by emergency physicians or trainees, against cardiology-performed or -interpreted echocardiography; the nine cohorts (seven prospective) came from Turkey (4), the US (3) and Iran (2). In the prespecified bivariate model, pooled sensitivity for a regional wall motion abnormality (RWMA) was 86% (95% CI 76 to 93) and specificity 85% (70 to 94); separately pooled LR+ was 5.42 (2.51 to 11.68) and LR− 0.17 (0.09 to 0.33). Study sensitivities ranged from 57% to 97% and specificities from 35% to 97%, RWMA prevalence from 8% to 72% (average about 45%), and four of nine studies were at high risk of bias; the authors rated certainty moderate.

Cangrelor vs Crushed Ticagrelor in MI With Cardiogenic Shock (DAPT-SHOCK-AMI, Double-Blind RCT, n=605): Primary Clinical Composite at 30 Days 38% vs 41%, Non-Inferiority Not Shown; Platelet Target Met in 100% vs 22% of a 20% Subsample

Motovska Z, Hlinomaz O, Mrozek J, et al.; DAPT-SHOCK-AMI study group. Cangrelor versus crushed ticagrelor in acute myocardial infarction complicated by cardiogenic shock (DAPT-SHOCK-AMI): a randomised, double-blind trial. Lancet. 2026 (online ahead of print, 7 November). doi: 10.1016/S0140-6736(26)00976-1. PMID: 42998670. NCT03551964; EudraCT 2018-002161-19. Source route: McMaster EvidenceAlerts (16 November); full text via ClinicalKey (read 20 November); declaration of interests from the PubMed record; no expert review used. Appraised from full text

Investigator-initiated double-blind, double-dummy trial at 29 hospitals in the Czech Republic, France, Germany, Poland and Slovakia (August 2018 to January 2024) in 605 adults with acute MI and cardiogenic shock (at least two of systolic BP below 90 mmHg without hypovolaemia, need for vasopressors or inotropes, and organ hypoperfusion) scheduled for primary PCI and not loaded with a P2Y12 inhibitor before admission; 55% had an out-of-hospital cardiac arrest and 61% were ventilated at randomisation. Patients received IV cangrelor (30 µg/kg bolus, then 4 µg/kg/min for at least 2 hours, with crushed ticagrelor 180 mg 30 minutes before the infusion ended) or crushed ticagrelor 180 mg. Death, MI or ischaemic stroke at 30 days occurred in 112 of 298 (38%) vs 126 of 307 (41%); non-inferiority against a 1-point margin was not shown (difference −4 points, 90% CI −10 to 3; P 0.13; HR 0.86, 95% CI 0.68 to 1.08), and death (37% vs 40%) made up almost all events. Platelet reactivity index below 50% at the end of PCI, measured only at sites able to transport samples, was met in 56 of 56 vs 15 of 68; stent thrombosis at 30 days was 0 vs 9 (3%) and 1-year death 44% vs 49% (p=0.044), but secondary endpoints were exploratory with no adjustment for multiplicity.

UK practice: NICE has no recommendation on cangrelor: NICE TA351 (terminated appraisal, 2015) says NICE is unable to recommend it because the company did not provide an evidence submission. Cangrelor is licensed in the UK (Kengrexal (cangrelor) SmPC (eMC, revised March 2026)), with aspirin, for adults having PCI who have not received an oral P2Y12 inhibitor and in whom oral P2Y12 therapy is not feasible or desirable. NICE NG185, acute coronary syndromes (2020) offers coronary angiography with follow-on primary PCI if indicated to people with STEMI and cardiogenic shock presenting within 12 hours (rec 1.1.7) and prasugrel with aspirin for primary PCI unless already anticoagulated (rec 1.1.11); it does not mention cangrelor or crushed tablets.

NEWS2 of 5 or More Triggering the Sepsis Hour-1 Bundle (NEWS-1 TRIPS, Pilot Stepped-Wedge Cluster RCT, 4 Hong Kong EDs): Feasible, but Bundle Compliance 56.1% and 30-Day Mortality 9.2% vs 3.9% (Adjusted RR 3.04, 0.78 to 11.86) in 200 Patients

Lam RPK, Hung KKC, Lui CT, et al. National Early Warning Score 2-guided Sepsis Hour-1 Bundle care for adults with suspected sepsis in the emergency department: a hybrid type 1 effectiveness-implementation stepped-wedge randomized controlled trial pilot study (NEWS-1 TRIPS). Crit Care Med. 2026 (online ahead of print, 12 November). doi:10.1097/CCM.0000000000007447. PMID: 43018426. NCT05731349. Source route: PubMed supplementary keyword sweep; open-access full text at the publisher (read 19 November); no expert review found. Appraised from full text

Pilot hybrid trial (March 2023 to May 2024) in four Hong Kong EDs that did not routinely use NEWS2: each began with standard care and switched, in random order at 2-month intervals, to NEWS-1 care, in which a NEWS2 of 5 or more in adults with a clinical diagnosis of infection needing admission triggered the Surviving Sepsis Campaign Hour-1 Bundle, with NEWS2 calculated by designated research nurses. Written or retrospective consent was required (no waiver), and the authors say older or very sick patients who could not consent were inevitably excluded: 203 of 5,855 screened were enrolled and 200 analysed (standard care 102, NEWS-1 98), and only 61.8% and 69.4% developed sepsis within 7 days. Overall Hour-1 Bundle compliance rose from 13.7% to 56.1%, short of the 70% target and the one progression criterion missed, and median time to antibiotics fell from 259 to 50 minutes. In the exploratory ITT analysis 30-day mortality was 3.9% vs 9.2% (absolute difference 5.3%, 95% CI −1.6 to 12.1; adjusted RR 3.04, 0.78 to 11.86), but the NEWS-1 group was sicker at enrolment (median NEWS2 7 vs 6, p=0.003); the authors estimate that a full stepped-wedge trial would need 8,555 patients.

Main concerns: the NEWS-1 group was sicker and came from a different site mix, and four clusters cannot separate the bundle from case mix or time, so the mortality excess may not be caused by the bundle (likely biased against NEWS-1); requiring written consent excluded the sickest patients (control mortality only 3.9%), so neither benefit nor harm in those who matter most can be judged (direction unknown); research nurses calculated NEWS2, which staff named as a barrier, so compliance without them would probably be lower than 56.1% (overstates feasibility) Strengths: registered, prespecified progression criteria, adjusted as well as unadjusted estimates, and a mixed-methods implementation evaluation (15 barriers, 13 facilitators) Not seen: the supplementary material and the full list of the 14 progression criteria and their thresholds.

UK practice: NICE NG253, suspected sepsis in people aged 16 or over (2025, updated September 2026), which replaced NG51, grades risk with NEWS2 in context: 7 or more high, 5 or 6 medium, 1 to 4 low (rec 1.6.2). For high risk it gives broad-spectrum IV antibiotics within 1 hour of calculating the NEWS2 score on initial ED assessment (rec 1.8.3). For medium risk it asks for a venous blood gas with lactate, blood cultures and other bloods with review by an FY2 or above within 1 hour (rec 1.8.16), suggests considering deferring antibiotics for up to 3 hours to reach a more specific diagnosis (rec 1.8.17), and treats medium risk with hypoperfusion (for example lactate over 2 mmol/L) as high risk (rec 1.8.20). RCP NEWS2 (December 2017) is endorsed by NHS England as the early warning system in hospitals in England. The NEWS-1 model would put NICE medium-risk patients on a 1-hour bundle, which NG253 does not recommend.

Automated Closed-Loop vs Manual Oxygen Titration in Emergency Care (SR/MA, 3 RCTs Pooled, 286 Patients): Mean Difference of 22.9% in Time in the Target Saturation Range, With No Patient-Centred Outcomes

Choonara L, Vally M, Mahomed A, Laher AE. Comparison of automated and manual oxygen titration in emergency care: a systematic review and meta-analysis. CJEM. 2026 (online ahead of print, 13 November). doi:10.1007/s43678-026-01306-z. PMID: 43024045. PROSPERO ID 1292267 (as printed). Source route: PubMed alert (EM journals) and PubMed sweep; open-access full text at the publisher (CC BY); no expert review found. Appraised from full text

Systematic review from Johannesburg (PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, the Cochrane Library and Google Scholar; adults, studies from 2000 onwards, first 24 hours of care; search dates not reported) comparing automated closed-loop with manual oxygen titration. Of 22 records, four studies were included, but only three RCTs (n=286) were pooled, one of which (Kirton 2024) is a conference abstract; the fourth, an observational study, was narrative only. Automated titration increased time within the target saturation range (mean difference 22.9%, 95% CI 9.6 to 36.2; I² 85.7%), or 29.6% (23.7 to 35.6; I² 0%) after leave-one-out removal of Kirton, whose manual arm was in range 87.1% of the time vs 51.8% in L’Her 2017. Three of the four studies tested the same commercial device (FreeO₂), run by investigator networks that included people involved in its development or validation; hypoxaemia and hyperoxia times are given for automated arms only, mortality did not differ, and the authors rate certainty moderate, downgrading only for inconsistency.

Electrical vs Pharmacological Cardioversion of Stable Recent-Onset AF (Italian Single-Centre Retrospective Cohort, n=575): Median ED Stay 114 vs 130 Minutes, 117 vs 151 by First Strategy, and 317 Minutes When Crossover Was Needed

Mariani A, Geniere Nigra S, Lorenzi L, Battaglini C, Francesconi R. Cardioversion of stable atrial fibrillation in the emergency department: electrical versus pharmacological in the era of overcrowding. J Emerg Med. 2026;91:208–217. doi:10.1016/j.jemermed.2026.10.007. PMID: 43025343. Source route: PubMed sweep of EM journals; full text via ClinicalKey (read 20 November); no expert review found. Appraised from full text

Retrospective cohort (January 2021 to May 2025) from one secondary-care ED in northern Italy of adults with dateable AF onset under 48 hours and a successful cardioversion: electrical under propofol (201) or midazolam-based sedation (27), each delivered by one emergency physician and one ED nurse, or flecainide (312), propafenone (15) or amiodarone (20). Of 827 identified, crossovers (121), failed cardioversions (26) and prolonged stays for other reasons (36) were among those excluded from the primary analysis, in which median ED stay was 114 minutes with electrical and 130 with pharmacological cardioversion (p=0.006); in 199 propensity-matched pairs, propofol vs flecainide was 106 vs 118 minutes (p=0.086). By first strategy (n=696, adding back the crossovers), median stay was 117 minutes for electrical (246) vs 151 for pharmacological (450), the first drug failed in 103 of 450, and crossover patients stayed a median 317 minutes. Transient respiratory depression followed 10 of 201 propofol cardioversions, with no intubation; clinicians chose the method, so patient selection, time of day and staffing may confound every comparison and the findings are hypothesis-generating.
