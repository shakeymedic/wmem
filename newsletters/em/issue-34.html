RCEM Eating disorders: pulse under 40, temperature under 35.5°C, SUSS 0–1, intake under 400 kcal/day or long QT are red flags; MEED recommends acute admission for anyone with a red flag.

KNOW FOR NEXT TIME

UK RCT Syncope (ASPIRED, n=2,234): 14-day ECG monitoring did not reduce syncope episodes at 1 year (IRR 0.89, 95% CI 0.68–1.18).

ECG OMI: in one cohort, 51.4% of angiographic occlusions were STEMI-negative and reached the cath lab far later.

COHORT Pre-alerted STEMI: only 60.1% of paramedic-suspected STEMIs were confirmed STEMI (Australian registry).

DIAGNOSTIC HFNO: a HACOR score of 5 or less at 60 minutes made failure unlikely (NPV 93.8%, single centre).

NICE Sickle cell: fat embolism syndrome added to CG143's list of complications of a painful crisis.

PAEDS Sore throat: IDSA now suggests clinical scores to decide who to test; not for under-3s.

NICE has changed its adult sepsis guideline on the strength of one trial, and this week’s lead sets out what the procalcitonin recommendation does and does not ask of an ED. Lead: NICE Adds Procalcitonin to Adult Sepsis Bloods at NEWS2 5 or More — Based on One Trial With a 3-Point Mortality Difference

National Institute for Health and Care Excellence. Suspected sepsis in people aged 16 or over: recognition, assessment and early management. NICE guideline NG253, published 19 November 2025, last updated 23 September 2026 (recommendations 1.8.2 and 1.8.16). nice.org.uk/guidance/ng253. Trial: Todd S, Euden J, Condie J, et al. PRONTO. Lancet Respir Med. 2026;14(5):417–431. PMID: 41881047. Source route: NICE website check. Appraised from full text

What changed: In September 2026 NICE updated recommendations 1.8.2 (high risk: suspected infection with NEWS2 of 7 or more, or a single parameter scoring 3 confirmed on medical review, or other clinical concern) and 1.8.16 (medium risk: NEWS2 5 or 6) to add “consider procalcitonin testing” to the venous blood tests. NICE also aligned its risk labels with NEWS2. The committee says the evidence came from 1 RCT, which found a clear mortality benefit but no effect on time to IV antibiotics, treatment duration, length of stay or ICU admission, and that the mechanism remains unexplained.

The trial behind it: PRONTO randomised 7,667 adults with suspected sepsis in 20 EDs in England and Wales to NEWS2-based usual care or procalcitonin-guided care (5,453 in the primary analysis). IV antibiotics by 3 hours did not change (48.4% vs 48.2%). Mortality at 28 days was lower with procalcitonin: 13.6% (372/2,738) vs 16.6% (450/2,715), adjusted risk difference −3.12 percentage points (90% CI −4.68 to −1.57; a 90% interval because the trial used a one-sided 5% test). NICE does not name the trial on the recommendations page; that PRONTO is the one RCT is our inference from the matching description, and we have not read NICE evidence review J. Recommendation

ASPIRED (UK RCT, n=2,234): 14-Day ECG Monitoring After Unexplained Syncope Did Not Reduce Syncope at 1 Year

Reed MJ, Goodacre S, Weir CJ, et al. Immediate Ambulatory Electrocardiographic Monitoring in Syncope. N Engl J Med. 2026 (published 31 August 2026). doi:10.1056/NEJMoa2605812. PMID: 42670977. ISRCTN10278811. Source route: PubMed sweep; discussed in The Resus Room Papers of October. Abstract only

Open-label RCT at 45 UK hospitals. Adults whose syncope remained unexplained after ED evaluation were randomised to immediate 14-day ambulatory ECG monitoring or each site’s standard care. The primary outcome was the mean number of patient-reported syncope episodes at 1 year: 1.37 ± 5.10 vs 1.58 ± 8.56 (incidence rate ratio 0.89, 95% CI 0.68 to 1.18). 1,970 of 2,234 (1,004 vs 966) were in the primary analysis. There were 49 adverse events with monitoring and 8 with standard care, with 1 serious event in each group.

Occlusion MI Missed by STEMI Criteria: 51.4% of Angiographic Occlusions Were STEMI-Negative and Reached Angiography Later

Occlusion Myocardial Infarction Missed by ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction (STEMI) Criteria: An Angiographic Emergency Department Cohort. J Emerg Med. 2026. doi:10.1016/j.jemermed.2026.09.017. PMID: 42854528. Source route: PubMed sweep; J Emerg Med Articles in Press alert. Abstract only

Retrospective single-centre cohort of 1,052 consecutive adults with an ED diagnosis of STEMI or NSTEMI who underwent angiography (December 2021 to October 2023). Occlusion MI (OMI) was defined as a culprit lesion with TIMI 0–2 flow, or over 70% stenosis with peak ED high-sensitivity troponin I above 10,000 ng/L. 837 met the OMI definition; 430 of 837 (51.4%) were STEMI-negative, and 51 of 458 STEMI patients (11.1%) had no occlusion. Angiography within 2 hours: 85.7% of STEMI-positive OMI vs 15.6% of STEMI-negative OMI.

Intubating the Septic Patient in ED (ANZEDAR): Haemodynamic Complications in 27.9% and Peri-Intubation Arrest in 4.6%

Characteristics, Practice Patterns, and Outcomes of Emergency Department Intubation for Sepsis: An ANZEDAR Registry Study. Emerg Med Australas. October 2026. doi:10.1111/1742-6723.70362. PMID: 42847126. Source route: PubMed sweep. Abstract only

Retrospective analysis of a prospective registry from 79 hospitals in Australia and New Zealand (2010 to March 2026). Of 11,289 intubations, 280 (2.5%) were for sepsis; 30 of 280 (10.7%) were children under 16. Waveform capnography was used less often in sepsis (87.5% vs 94.1%), with particularly low use in infants.

TRICYCLE (Phase II RCT, n=79): Early Angiotensin II plus Vasopressin plus Noradrenaline in Septic Shock Improved Physiology, Not Mortality

SGA vs Intubation in Adult OHCA (SR/MA, 13 Studies): No Significant Difference in Survival (I² 80%); RCTs and Cohorts Point in Opposite Directions for ROSC

Supraglottic airway versus endotracheal intubation in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Resusc Plus. 2026. doi:10.1016/j.resplu.2026.101491. PMID: 42831128. CRD420261337076. Source route: PubMed sweep. Abstract only

Thirteen studies (246,659 patients; randomised and observational). Survival to hospital discharge did not differ (RR 0.96, 95% CI 0.82 to 1.12; I² 80%). SGA was associated with less ROSC overall (RR 0.86, 95% CI 0.77 to 0.96), but the direction reversed by design: lower ROSC with SGA in observational studies (RR 0.81, 95% CI 0.73 to 0.89) and higher ROSC with SGA in the two RCTs (RR 1.09, 95% CI 1.03 to 1.16). The authors call the findings hypothesis-generating.

  • RR 0.96 SURVIVAL TO DISCHARGE (0.82 TO 1.12)
  • RR 0.81 ROSC, OBSERVATIONAL
  • RR 1.09 ROSC, 2 RCTS (1.03 TO 1.16)

Suction vs Water Seal After Chest Drain for Trauma (SR/MA, 5 RCTs, n=404): Shorter Drain Time and Stay With Suction, From Trials at High Risk of Bias

Comparative effectiveness of suction versus water seal following tube thoracostomy in traumatic pneumothorax and hemothorax: an updated systematic review and meta-analysis. Am J Emerg Med. 2026;109:250–258. doi:10.1016/j.ajem.2026.07.055. PROSPERO CRD420261392312. Source route: candidate PDF and PubMed alert (EM journals, 30 September). Appraised from full text

Five RCTs (404 patients) of suction (−5 to −20 cmH2O) vs water seal after tube thoracostomy for traumatic pneumothorax or haemothorax. Suction shortened chest drain duration (mean difference −2.95 days, 95% CI −4.58 to −1.31; I² 77%) and hospital stay (−3.98 days, 95% CI −5.70 to −2.27; I² 68%), and improved full lung expansion (OR 4.86, 95% CI 1.70 to 13.88). No significant differences in recurrent pneumothorax, re-intervention or retained haemothorax. All trials were at high risk of bias, mainly from lack of blinding.

A Heart Rate That Rises Before Discharge: HR Increase of 10 or More Linked to Serious 7-Day Bouncebacks (aOR 3.10)

Paramedic-Suspected STEMI (8,077 Visits): STEMI Confirmed in 60.1%; Non-Cardiac Final Diagnoses Carried the Highest Long-Term Mortality (HR 1.75)

Lankaputhra M, Bishop A, Wijaya J, et al. Final Diagnoses and Outcomes After Paramedic-Suspected ST-Segment Elevation Myocardial Infarction. Ann Emerg Med. Published online 6 October 2026. doi:10.1016/j.annemergmed.2026.07.027. PMID: 42841835. Source route: PubMed sweep; Ann Emerg Med RSS. Abstract only

Retrospective statewide registry cohort (Victorian Ambulance STEMI Quality Initiative, January 2020 to June 2025) of 8,077 paramedic-suspected STEMI visits with hospital follow-up. Core Revision — ECG: STEMI Equivalents — Patterns to Recognise When STEMI Criteria Are Not Met

Occlusion patterns, right and posterior leads, and when the ECG is normal — FRCEM

Exam goal: Recognise the ECG patterns that indicate acute coronary occlusion without meeting STEMI millimetre criteria, and know which extra leads to record.

1. THE PATTERNS

PATTERNWHAT YOU SEEWHAT IT MEANS
De Winter1–3 mm upsloping ST depression at the J point in V3–V6 with tall, symmetric T waves; no anterior ST elevationAcute occlusion, classically proximal LAD (about 2% of such occlusions); can evolve into ST elevation
Hyperacute T wavesBroad, tall T waves out of proportion to the QRS in a coronary territoryEarly occlusion that may precede ST elevation; part of the OMI framework
Posterior MIHorizontal ST depression V1–V3, tall broad R waves, upright T waves, R/S over 1 in V2Record V7–V9; 0.5 mm ST elevation there is diagnostic. Accompanies 15–20% of STEMIs
RV infarctionInferior STEMI with ST elevation in V1, or III greater than IIRecord V4R (sensitivity 88%, specificity 78%); preload dependent, so avoid nitrates and give fluid for hypotension
LBBB or paced rhythm (Smith-modified Sgarbossa)Concordant ST elevation 1 mm or more; concordant ST depression 1 mm or more in V1–V3; discordant ST elevation 25% or more of the preceding S waveOriginal Sgarbossa is specific but only about 36% sensitive; the modified ratio criterion improves this
Wellens (type A biphasic, type B deep symmetric T inversion V2–V3)Seen when pain-free; little or no ST elevation; preserved R wavesCritical proximal LAD stenosis; high risk of anterior MI within days to weeks; do not stress test
ST elevation in aVR with widespread ST depressionDepression maximal V4–V6, with aVR elevationClassically left main or severe multivessel disease; recent data show it predicts severe disease and mortality but poorly predicts an acute occlusion
The normal ECG in ACSNo diagnostic changeA normal ECG does not exclude MI; use a validated troponin pathway (see St Emlyn’s item)
