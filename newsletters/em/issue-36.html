KNOW FOR NEXT TIME

SR/MA Induction agents (21 RCTs, n=6,031): ketamine caused more peri-intubation instability than etomidate (RR 1.31, 95% CI 1.15 to 1.48), with no difference in mortality (RR 0.96).

SR/MA Steroids in septic shock: against placebo, hydrocortisone 200–299 mg/day alone did not reduce 28-day mortality (OR 0.92, 95% CI 0.81 to 1.04) and hydrocortisone plus fludrocortisone did (OR 0.78, 0.64 to 0.96), but the two regimens did not differ significantly from each other (OR 1.17, 0.92 to 1.48).

SR/MA DKA fluids: two-bag systems were associated with less hypoglycaemia (9.2% vs 23.9%) in mostly non-randomised studies; JBDS-IP already runs 10% glucose alongside saline.

COHORT Hyperkalaemia: coded arrest, VT or VF within 48 h was 6.68% at potassium 7.0 or more vs under 1% in matched controls; a risk marker, not proof of cause.

RCT Late thrombolysis (HOPE): alteplase at 4.5 to 24 h with perfusion mismatch gave mRS 0–1 in 40.3% vs 26.3%, with sICH 3.8% vs 0.5%.

PAEDS Newborn resuscitation (Cochrane): starting in air may reduce death before discharge (RR 0.72, 95% CI 0.57 to 0.91; low certainty).

Recognise it. The 2026 AHA/ACC guideline replaces “massive” and “submassive” with categories A to E. Category D1 is transient hypotension (such as syncope), D2 is normotensive shock (raised lactate or acute kidney injury despite a normal blood pressure), E1 is persistent hypotension with cardiogenic shock and E2 is refractory shock or arrest. Hunsicker et al list the guideline’s markers (lactate above 2 mmol/L, rising creatinine, urine under 0.5 mL/kg/h, altered mental state, cardiac index 2.2 L/min/m² or less) and add capillary refill over 3 seconds, mottling and echo. UK practice uses ESC 2019 terms (Schramm); NG158 uses only stable and unstable.

Opinion, not guidance (EMCrit 424 and our reading), a DGH pathway: (1) In PE with RV strain, check and repeat lactate, capillary refill, mottling, urine output and an RV view on echo. STORM-PE 90-Day Outcomes (RCT, n=100): After Catheter Clot Aspiration, Patients Walked Further (479 vs 368 m), but Only About Two-Thirds of Each Arm Did the Walk Test

Rosovsky RP, Konstantinides SV, Weinberg I, et al. Mechanical Thrombectomy Versus Anticoagulation Alone in Intermediate-High Risk PE: Improved Functional 90-Day Outcomes in STORM-PE RCT. JACC Cardiovasc Interv. 2026;19(20):2775–2790. doi:10.1016/j.jcin.2026.07.042. PMID: 42836810. NCT05684796. Sponsored and funded by Penumbra. Source route: PDF via BMA Library (supplied by Jake). Primary results covered in Issues 7 and 12. Appraised from full text

STORM-PE randomised 100 adults with intermediate-high-risk PE at 22 sites (USA, Canada, Poland, New Zealand) to computer-assisted vacuum thrombectomy (Lightning Flash 16F) plus anticoagulation (n=47) or anticoagulation alone (n=53); the primary endpoint, RV/LV ratio change at 48 hours, was reported earlier. This paper reports secondary outcomes to 90 days, without adjustment for multiple comparisons. Among those who did the test, 6-minute walk distance at 90 days was 478.7 ± 159.5 m vs 368.0 ± 127.8 m (P=0.003; CI for the difference not reported), and 97.4% (37/38) vs 75.6% (34/45) were NYHA class I (OR 11.96, 95% CI 1.47 to 97.61). Quality-of-life scores, symptomatic recurrence and mortality did not differ significantly; there were 4 deaths (8.5%) with thrombectomy vs 1 (1.9%) with anticoagulation, none judged device- or procedure-related.

ECMO Plus Catheter Clot Removal vs ECMO Alone in High-Risk PE (SR/MA, 4 Cohorts, n=2,824): No Proven Mortality Benefit, and Only 47 Patients Could Be Pooled

Hasan B, Aldin ST, Buda KG, et al. Extracorporeal Membrane Oxygenation Combined With Mechanical Thrombectomy Versus Extracorporeal Membrane Oxygenation Alone for High-Risk Pulmonary Embolism: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis. J Cardiothorac Vasc Anesth. 2026 (online 31 August). doi:10.1053/j.jvca.2026.08.165. PMID: 42786027. Source route: PDF via BMA Library (supplied by Jake). Appraised from full text

Mayo Clinic reviewers searched PubMed and Embase to 6 October 2025 for adults with high-risk PE on VA-ECMO, comparing ECMO plus catheter thrombectomy with ECMO alone; the primary outcome was in-hospital mortality. Four retrospective cohorts (n=2,824) qualified, but only two single-centre studies (n=47) could be pooled: OR 0.36 (95% CI 0.01 to 13.59; I² 69%). The two larger studies were not pooled because one was adjusted and one mixed catheter with surgical embolectomy: a US national database (n=2,285; mechanical reperfusion, including surgery) gave adjusted OR 0.75 (0.47 to 1.19), and the international CATAPULTE cohort gave 90-day mortality of 32% vs 39% after propensity matching (HR 0.68, 0.45 to 1.03). Major bleeding did not differ (adjusted OR 0.93, 0.58 to 1.48), and certainty was very low for every mortality outcome.

Induction Agents for Intubating the Critically Ill (NMA, 21 RCTs, n=6,031): More Peri-Intubation Instability With Ketamine Than Etomidate (RR 1.31), No Difference in Mortality

McDougall G, Forestell B, Sadeghirad B, et al. Induction Agents for Tracheal Intubation of Critically Ill Patients: A Systematic Review and Network Meta-Analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials. Chest. 2026 (published online 18 August 2026). doi:10.1016/j.chest.2026.07.5239. PMID: 42612901. PROSPERO CRD42024600739. Source route: JournalFeed Article of the Day 27 Oct; PDF via BMA Library (supplied by Jake; journal proof). Appraised from full text

Network meta-analysis of 21 RCTs (6,031 critically ill patients intubated in the ED, ICU, operating room or prehospital; 6 ED trials), searched to 10 December 2025 and including the 2,365-patient RSI trial; no primary outcome was named. Most patients received etomidate (2,749) or ketamine (2,600), and no arm used propofol alone. Against etomidate, ketamine increased haemodynamic instability during intubation (mostly systolic BP under 90 mmHg; RR 1.31, 95% CI 1.15 to 1.48), as did ketamine-opioid and midazolam-opioid combinations, without a mortality difference. Etomidate increased adrenal suppression; a 1:1 ketamine-propofol mix looked best for instability but rests on 2 trials and 129 patients.

Kwon S, Kwak SG, Hong HL. Comparative efficacy and safety of hydrocortisone-based regimens in septic shock: A network meta-analysis. J Crit Care. 2027;97:155750 (online 22 September 2026). doi:10.1016/j.jcrc.2026.155750. PMID: 42784878. PROSPERO CRD420251015672. Source route: JournalFeed EM Speed Read 26 Oct; PDF via BMA Library (supplied by Jake). Appraised from full text

Network meta-analysis of 21 RCTs (7,908 adults with septic shock, or severe sepsis in older trials) comparing placebo, hydrocortisone 200–299 mg/day (H200), 300 mg/day or more (H300), and H200 plus fludrocortisone 50, 100 or 200 µg/day. For the primary outcome, 28-day mortality (14 trials, 7,303 participants), H200 alone did not differ from placebo, whereas H200 plus fludrocortisone 50 µg and H300 were associated with lower mortality (table). Two-Bag vs One-Bag Fluids in DKA (SR/MA, 21 Studies, Adults and Children): Hypoglycaemia 9.2% vs 23.9% (RR 0.50), From Almost Entirely Non-Randomised Data

Srikrishnaraj A, Souter AR, Woods N, et al. Two-bag Versus One-bag Method for Adult and Pediatric Diabetic Ketoacidosis Management. Ann Emerg Med. 2026;87(3):346–364. doi:10.1016/j.annemergmed.2025.07.032. PMID: 40913602. PROSPERO CRD42024541648. Source route: PDF via BMA Library (supplied by Jake); open access (CC BY). Appraised from full text

Systematic review of hospital DKA treatment comparing two bags (same electrolytes, different dextrose content, run together and titrated to glucose) with one bag (swapped for a dextrose fluid as glucose falls). It included 21 studies (4 RCTs, 16 retrospective cohorts, 1 quality-improvement study): 9 adult (3,329 visits) and 12 paediatric (1,385 visits). For the primary outcome of hypoglycaemia (glucose under 3.89 mmol/L [70 mg/dL]), pooled from 14 studies (all but one non-randomised), rates were 9.2% (215/2,345) with two bags vs 23.9% (473/1,975) with one bag (RR 0.50, 95% CI 0.41 to 0.59; I² 51.8%). In adults, hypokalaemia was less frequent (RR 0.84, 95% CI 0.76 to 0.93).
