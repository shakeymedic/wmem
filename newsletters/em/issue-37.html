WORTH KNOWING ON SHIFT

RCT Ventilation during CPR (crossover RCT, 99 intubated adults in ongoing CPR after the decision to stop had been made): the ventilator gave a 146.2 mL lower measured tidal volume than bagging (95% CI −187.0 to −105.5), and 31.3% of bagged medians exceeded 10 mL/kg.

UK Thoracotomy (TETRiS, 116 UK cases): ROSC 43.1%, survival to discharge 6.9%, and no survivor beyond 10 minutes of arrest.

RCT Renal colic (n=251, unregistered): 30-minute success 76.7% with IV ibuprofen, 74.4% with dexketoprofen and 66.3% with IV paracetamol (p=0.280); NSAIDs stay first line (NICE NG118).

COHORT FAST: 24.8% of FAST-negative trauma patients still had a severe internal injury (including pelvic fractures and haemothorax, which FAST is not designed to find); a negative scan does not exclude it.

KNOW FOR NEXT TIME

UK Ramping (UK EDs): at sites that held patients in ambulances, each 5-point rise in the share of escalation care held there was associated with higher 28-day mortality (adjusted HR 1.021, 95% CI 1.002 to 1.041); any use vs none was not (HR 0.957); observational.

DIAGNOSTIC PE rules after the CTPA decision: YEARS kept 96.5% sensitivity but would have spared imaging in only 13.8% of the 398 patients with a D-dimer.

SR/MA Bicarbonate (4 ICU RCTs, n=1,544): no clear mortality reduction (RR 0.94, 95% CrI 0.73 to 1.22) but probably less renal replacement therapy (RR 0.74, 95% CrI 0.59 to 0.98).

DIAGNOSTIC Head injury blood test: GFAP/UCH-L1 sensitivity 91% (95% CI 59 to 100%) but specificity 22%; not a substitute for the NICE NG232 CT rules.

COHORT Cannabinoid hyperemesis: 72-hour return 8.2% after haloperidol vs 15.1% after ondansetron (OR 0.50, 95% CI 0.41 to 0.62); off-label in the UK, ECG first.

PAEDS Video laryngoscopy: crude first-pass success 72.2% vs 69.0% with direct laryngoscopy (pooled RR 1.11, 95% CI 1.06 to 1.16), very low certainty.

0/1 h vs 0/3 h Troponin Pathways (IPD Meta-Analysis, 5 RCTs, 110,933 Presentations): 30-Day Death or Type 1 MI 0.8% vs 1.0%, With No Clear Gain in ED Length of Stay

Boeddinghaus J, Kunz M, Lopez-Ayala P, et al. Performance of 0/1 h versus 0/3 h pathways in patients with suspected myocardial infarction: a systematic review and individual participant data meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials. Lancet. 2026 (online 29 October). doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(26)01767-8. PMID: 42956796. PROSPERO CRD420261383452. Source route: PubMed (found while resolving EMA Daily 3 Nov); abstract only, no free full text, and no JournalFeed, SGEM or other commentary found by 5 November. Background: the 0/1 h vs 0/3 h RCT reported by JournalFeed on 23 July was covered in Issue 22 (24 July 2026). Abstract only

Two-stage random-effects IPD meta-analysis of all five RCTs comparing a 0/1 h with a 0/3 h hs-troponin pathway (55,489 vs 55,444 presentations; every trial supplied IPD). The primary safety outcome, all-cause death or new type 1 MI within 30 days, occurred in 446 (0.8%) vs 562 (1.0%) presentations (pooled OR 0.83, 95% CI 0.45 to 1.53; I² 62.9%). The primary efficacy outcomes did not clearly differ: median ED length of stay 314 vs 308 minutes (geometric mean ratio 1.03, 95% CI 0.85 to 1.25; I² 99.2%) and direct ED discharge 67.0% vs 65.8% (OR 1.31, 95% CI 0.79 to 2.16; I² 97.8%). The authors conclude that both pathways had low short-term event rates but that the effect on flow may not transfer uniformly across health systems.

PE Rules After the CTPA Has Been Ordered (Indian ED Cohort, n=408): YEARS Sensitivity 96.5%, but Only 13.8% Could Have Avoided Imaging

Pannu AK, Saroch A, Bhatia V, et al. Performance of pulmonary embolism diagnostic strategies in patients undergoing computed tomography pulmonary angiography. Intern Emerg Med. 2026 (online 28 October). doi:10.1007/s11739-026-04573-x. PMID: 42955693. Source route: PubMed sweep; abstract only, no free full text, and no secondary commentary found by 5 November. Abstract only

Prospective study of consecutive ED patients aged 13 or over in whom a clinician had already decided to request CTPA for suspected PE (authors from PGIMER, Chandigarh, India; number of centres not stated in the abstract). After that decision, investigators applied the modified Wells and simplified revised Geneva scores and the YEARS and PEGeD strategies, with segmental or more proximal PE on CTPA as the primary outcome. Significant PE was found in 144 of 408 (35.3%); Wells discriminated better than Geneva (AUC 0.725, 95% CI 0.671 to 0.779, vs 0.656, 95% CI 0.598 to 0.714), but 21.6% of Wells “PE unlikely” patients had significant PE. In 398 patients with a D-dimer, YEARS and PEGeD each had 96.5% sensitivity but would have spared imaging in only 13.8% and 13.6%.

Ventilator vs Bag in Intubated Adults During CPR (Crossover RCT, n=99): The Ventilator Gave a 146 mL Lower Measured Tidal Volume and Missed Non-Inferiority, While 31.3% of Bagged Medians Exceeded 10 mL/kg

Vinay S, Manu Ayyan S, Amrithanand VT, et al. Mechanical ventilation vs bag-valve ventilation during cardiopulmonary resuscitation: a randomized crossover noninferiority trial. Resusc Plus. 2026;32:101530. doi:10.1016/j.resplu.2026.101530. PMID: 42985663. CTRI/2024/08/072738. Source route: PubMed sweep; PMC full text (PMC13733466). Appraised from full text

Single-centre randomised two-period crossover trial in an Indian tertiary ED in real patients, not manikins: 104 intubated adults in ongoing CPR were enrolled only after the treating physician had decided termination criteria were met (99 completed both periods; 46.5% out-of-hospital arrests). Each received 1 minute of ventilator breaths (volume control, 8 mL/kg predicted body weight, rate 10, PEEP 0, flow trigger off) and 1 minute of bag-valve ventilation through the tube (1,600 mL bag, visible chest rise, no feedback) in random order with compressions continuing. For the primary outcome, the difference in each patient’s median device-calculated inspired tidal volume (margin −150 mL), the adjusted mean difference was −146.2 mL (95% CI −187.0 to −105.5), so non-inferiority was not shown, and no clinical outcomes were measured.

UK practice: The RCUK Adult advanced life support guidelines (2025) say that after intubation you ventilate at 10 per minute without pausing compressions. TETRiS (UK National Evaluation, n=116 Resuscitative Thoracotomies): ROSC in 43.1%, Survival to Discharge in 6.9%, and No Survivor Beyond 10 Minutes of Arrest

Ramage L, Tucker H, Marsden ME, et al; TETRiS Collaborative. Traumatic Emergency Thoracotomy for Resuscitation in Shock (TETRiS): a national service evaluation of resuscitative thoracotomy practice outside the operating theatre. Resuscitation. 2026 (online 28 October 2026): 111371. doi:10.1016/j.resuscitation.2026.111371. PMID: 42950975. Source route: JournalFeed EM SpeedRead 5 Nov; PubMed abstract (closed access, not available to us in full); no published commentary found. Abstract only

Prospective 12-month service evaluation of resuscitative thoracotomy across 20 UK HEMS services and 26 major trauma centre EDs (PHOTON, TERN and NaTRIC networks), with no comparator group. Of 116 thoracotomies, 82 (70.7%) were done on scene; the median age was 30 years and 81 (69.8%) had penetrating injury. Suspected tamponade was the commonest indication (72, 62.1%), but the operative finding was exsanguinating haemorrhage in 82 (70.7%). ROSC occurred in 50 (43.1%) and 8 (6.9%) survived to discharge; survival was better with tamponade than exsanguination and with ED than prehospital thoracotomy (shorter arrests, better physiology), and nobody survived beyond 10 minutes of arrest.

Signs of Life in Traumatic Cardiac Arrest (Kanto Registry, n=462): 10.2% With Any Electrical Activity vs 2.5% Without Survived Beyond the ED, and 1 Patient Had a Good Neurological Outcome

Retrospective descriptive analysis of the prospective SOS-KANTO 2017 cardiac arrest registry (42 hospitals in Tokyo and its suburbs, September 2019 to March 2021): 462 adults already in traumatic arrest when the ambulance arrived, grouped by three signs of life at scene (pupillary light reflex, agonal gasping, and any rhythm other than asystole on the first monitor). Only 1 patient (0.2%) had a Cerebral Performance Category of 1–2 at 30 days, and there was no adjusted analysis.

Renal Colic, IV Ibuprofen vs Dexketoprofen vs Paracetamol (Unregistered RCT, n=251): 30-Minute Success 76.7%, 74.4% and 66.3%, Not Significantly Different

Opioid-free analgesia for acute renal colic: a randomized, double-blind, head-to-head comparison of intravenous ibuprofen, dexketoprofen trometamol and paracetamol in the emergency department. Intern Emerg Med. 2026 (published online 30 October 2026). doi:10.1007/s11739-026-04579-5. PMID: 42961621. Trial not registered (stated in the abstract). Source route: PubMed sweep (abstract and publisher page); no blog or journal commentary found on 5 November 2026. Abstract only
