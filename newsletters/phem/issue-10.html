BOTTOM LINE UP FRONT — NOVEMBER 2026

ACT ON THIS NOW

Nothing this month changes practice on its own. The action points at the end are checks against existing UK guidance (RCUK 2025, NICE NG39, JESIP, Safer Prehospital Anaesthesia 2026).

WORTH KNOWING ON SHIFT

UK Thoracotomy (TETRiS, abstract): 116 UK resuscitative thoracotomies, 70.7% on scene: ROSC 43.1% and survival to discharge 6.9%; none of the 8 survivors had been in arrest for more than 10 minutes. Too few survivors to change the RCUK limit of under 15 minutes.

OBS TXA: fibrinolytic phenotype is a post-randomisation subgroup; keep giving TXA to bleeding major trauma within 3 hours per NICE NG39.

DX GFAP after RSI (UK HEMS, n=65): sensitivity 97.0% but specificity 53.1% for intracranial haemorrhage; a positive result barely shifts probability (LR+ 2.07, calculated).

PAEDS Births before arrival (France, n=3,473): 62.7% of newborns with a recorded temperature were hypothermic on scene; start thermal care at once and measure the temperature.

REVIEW Chemical agents: decontaminate first; atropine with early pralidoxime for nerve agents; rocuronium rather than suxamethonium; empiric hydroxocobalamin for suspected cyanide.

KNOW FOR NEXT TIME

OBS Dispatcher CPR barriers (Sweden, n=3,263): 43.7% of recognised arrests then met a barrier, most often a caller unable to move the patient; 30-day survival 6.5% vs 14.8% (adjusted RR 0.57, 95% CrI 0.46 to 0.71).

PAEDS Rescue breaths (Japan, non-cardiac OHCA, n=154,137): linked to lower 30-day mortality in children (adjusted RR 0.88, 0.83 to 0.94) and after drowning, with no clear difference in adults.

OBS ECPR (Göttingen model): 20.5% met local criteria at EMS arrival; modelled collapse-to-flow was 37 minutes on scene vs 75 minutes in hospital.

UK Ramping (131 UK EDs): each 5-point rise in escalation care held in ambulances was associated with a 2.1% higher 28-day mortality hazard (HR 1.021, 1.002 to 1.041); fragile and post hoc.

OBS HEMS airway (Poland, n=8,980): a supraglottic airway as the final airway had higher unadjusted odds of death at handover, but confounding by indication makes this uninterpretable as device harm.

RCT Chest pain conveyance (PreHEART, n=5,170, abstract): non-inferior for 3-day serious events (win ratio 1.01, 0.89 to 1.16) with more patients left at home (34.6% vs 24.5%); not for UK use yet.

OBS Older adults in traumatic arrest (France, n=5,595): 30-day survival 0.5% vs 1.2% under 65; age alone is not a reason to withhold resuscitation.

PAEDS Paediatric OHCA (ANZ, n=3,139): 10.0% survived; each extra minute of EMS response was linked to 4% lower odds of survival.

PAEDS Video laryngoscopy in children (18 studies): higher first-pass success (RR 1.11, 1.06 to 1.16) at very low certainty; the two prehospital studies were inconclusive.

Around it sit four UK papers: the national TETRiS evaluation of resuscitative thoracotomy, a HEMS study of point-of-care GFAP after RSI, the UNCORKED analysis of ambulance ramping and mortality, and AACE’s new position on public bleed kits. Dispatcher Training, Volunteer App and Audit to Raise Bystander CPR (French Stepped-Wedge Cluster RCT, n=1,684): Bystander CPR Rose From 61.4% to 70.9%, but 30-Day Survival Did Not Differ Significantly

Debaty G, Ribes S, Ray P, et al. A multifaceted intervention to increase bystander CPR rates after out-of-hospital cardiac arrest in France: a stepped-wedge, cluster-randomised, controlled trial. Lancet Public Health. 2026;11:e878–e888. doi:10.1016/S2468-2667(26)00200-8. PMID: 42901186. ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03633370. Source route: PDF via BMA Library (open access, CC BY 4.0). Appraised from full text

Fifteen French physician-staffed EMS agencies were randomised to the order in which they switched from usual care to a three-part intervention: dispatcher training in arrest recognition and scripted compression-only telephone CPR, activation of trained volunteers through the SAUV Life geolocation app, and monthly audit and feedback. The primary outcome was bystander-initiated CPR before EMS arrival in consecutive adults with non-traumatic OHCA; the comparator was each agency’s own control period. Two agencies withdrew and were replaced and one enrolled no one, so 14 agencies enrolled 2,160 patients; the intention-to-treat analysis was limited to the 12 originally randomised agencies (686 control and 998 intervention patients), bystander CPR occurred in 61.4% vs 70.9% (adjusted OR 1.54, 95% CI 1.09 to 2.19), mostly through more telephone-directed CPR (51.3% to 61.9%); volunteer CPR rose from 0.4% to 10.9%. The effect lost significance after adjusting for agency-specific time trends (OR 1.38, 95% CI 0.98 to 1.94), and no clinical outcome differed significantly.

Barriers After the Call Handler Recognises Cardiac Arrest (Swedish Registry-Linked 112 Calls, n=3,263): Present in 43.7% of Calls, With Later Bystander CPR (Primary Outcome) and Lower 30-Day Survival (Adjusted RR 0.57, 95% CrI 0.46 to 0.71)

Boberg E, Jonsson M, Riva G, et al. Barriers to dispatcher-assisted CPR are associated with delayed bystander CPR and worse outcomes in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest: a retrospective cohort study. Resuscitation. 2026;228:111305. doi:10.1016/j.resuscitation.2026.111305. PMID: 42727702. Source route: PDF via BMA Library (open access, CC BY 4.0). Appraised from full text

Retrospective cohort of transcribed 112 emergency calls pooled from five Swedish OHCA studies (2017–2025), linked to the Swedish Registry for Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation, including EMS-treated arrests that the dispatcher had recognised and where CPR was not already under way. Reviewers blinded to outcome coded barriers arising after recognition (caller not with the patient, hung up or left the phone, language barrier, overly distraught caller, unable to move the patient, refusal), compared with barrier-free calls; the primary objective was time to bystander CPR and the secondary outcomes were ROSC and 30-day survival. Barriers occurred in 1,428 of 3,263 calls (43.7%), most often inability to move the patient (55.8% of barrier calls), and bystander CPR started in 68.8% vs 88.3%, with a median 270 vs 156 seconds to first compressions; each extra barrier added delay (103, 174 and 225 seconds for one, two and three or more barriers). Crude 30-day survival was 6.5% vs 14.8% (adjusted RR 0.57, 95% credible interval 0.46 to 0.71) and ROSC 32.4% vs 39.9% (adjusted RR 0.89, 0.81 to 0.98).

Rescue Breaths vs Compression-Only Bystander CPR in Non-Cardiac OHCA (All-Japan Utstein Registry, n=154,137): Adjusted RR for 30-Day Mortality 0.98 Overall, 0.88 in Children and 0.94 After Drowning

Iida Y, Obara T, Nojima T, et al. Rescue-breathing versus chest compression-only bystander cardiopulmonary resuscitation in non-cardiac out-of-hospital cardiac arrest: a nationwide retrospective cohort study. Resuscitation. 2026;228:111332. doi:10.1016/j.resuscitation.2026.111332. PMID: 42790858. Source route: PDF via BMA Library (open access, CC BY 4.0). Appraised from full text

Nationwide registry cohort (2012–2023) of non-cardiac, non-traumatic OHCA given bystander chest compressions with rescue-breathing status recorded; 20,124 (13.1%) had compressions plus rescue breaths and 134,013 compression-only CPR. The primary outcome was 30-day mortality, estimated with modified Poisson regression adjusted for age, sex, witnessed arrest, dispatcher instruction, bystander AED use, presumed cause and call-to-EMS-contact time (complete cases 61.2%). Crude mortality was 89.8% vs 94.1% (adjusted RR 0.98, 95% CI 0.98 to 0.99), so much of the crude difference of 4.3 per 100 disappeared after adjustment. The association was clearest in children under 18 (60.4% vs 78.3%; adjusted RR 0.88, 0.83 to 0.94), after drowning (adjusted RR 0.94, 0.92 to 0.96) and in children after drowning (adjusted RR 0.67, 0.55 to 0.82), with no clear difference in adults or older people.

Point-of-Care GFAP in Non-Traumatic Coma After Prehospital RSI (East Anglian Air Ambulance, n=65): Sensitivity 97.0% but Specificity 53.1% for Intracranial Haemorrhage on CT

Price J, Lachowycz K, Palti G, et al. Prehospital Point-of-Care GFAP for Detection of Intracranial Hemorrhage in Acute Nontraumatic Coma. JAMA Netw Open. 2026;9(10):e2639337. doi:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.39337. PMID: 42879151. Source route: PMC full text (PMC13673314; open access, CC BY-NC-ND); the supplementary threshold table was not available. Appraised from full text

Diagnostic study of consecutive adults (16 or older) with non-traumatic coma (GCS 12 or less) who had prehospital rapid sequence induction (RSI) with suspected intracranial haemorrhage (ICH), attended by a UK physician-paramedic HEMS between January 2025 and April 2026. Whole-blood glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP) and UCH-L1 were measured on scene with the i-STAT Alinity traumatic brain injury cartridge (results within 15 minutes), against CT as the reference standard read blind to the biomarker. Of 65 patients (median GCS 4), 33 (50.8%) had ICH; GFAP above the manufacturer’s 65 pg/mL threshold had sensitivity 97.0% (95% CI 84.2% to 99.9%), specificity 53.1% (34.7% to 70.9%) and AUC 0.94 (0.87 to 0.99), and the one false negative was an isolated basal subarachnoid haemorrhage. UCH-L1 discriminated worse than chance (AUC 0.30), and calling either test positive gave sensitivity 100.0% (89.4% to 100.0%) at specificity 15.6% (5.3% to 32.8%).

Ambulance Ramping vs Corridor Care, UNCORKED Secondary Analysis (131 UK EDs, 18,744 Admissions): Each 5-Point Rise in the Share of Escalation Care Held in Ambulances Was Linked to 2.1% Higher 28-Day Mortality Hazard

McHenry RD, Birse F, Clarke B, Roberts T, on behalf of the Trainee Emergency Research Network. Ambulance or corridor? The association between site-level use of ambulance ‘ramping’ as Emergency Department escalation areas and 28-day mortality in admitted patients: a secondary analysis of the UNCORKED study. Scand J Trauma Resusc Emerg Med. 2026 (article in press). doi:10.1186/s13049-026-01710-5. PMID not yet assigned (PubMed checked by DOI, 1 Nov 2026). ISRCTN16396025. Source route: open-access PDF. Appraised from full text

Post hoc analysis of a prospective snapshot study of 131 type 1 EDs in England, Wales and Northern Ireland (five snapshots, March 2025; Scotland had no patient-level data), including 18,744 admitted patients aged 16 or over. The exposure was each site’s share of all escalation-area time spent in ambulances waiting more than 15 minutes to hand over; 82 sites (62.6%) used ambulances this way. The primary outcome was 28-day all-cause mortality (6.6% overall), in a Cox model adjusted for age, sex, trauma designation, admission rate and the site’s total escalation burden. Any ambulance use was not associated with mortality (HR 0.957, 95% CI 0.808 to 1.134), but among users each 5-point rise in the ambulance share was (HR 1.021, 95% CI 1.002 to 1.041); crude mortality was 8.9% at sites above 15% vs 6.4% at sites with no ambulance holding.

  • 8.9% vs 6.4% 28-DAY MORTALITY, INDEX >15% VS NO AMBULANCE USE (UNADJUSTED)
  • HR 1.021 PER 5-POINT RISE IN AMBULANCE SHARE (95% CI 1.002 TO 1.041)
  • HR 0.957 ANY AMBULANCE USE (95% CI 0.808 TO 1.134)
